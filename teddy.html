Benchmarking

OVERVIEW

When reviewing existing solutions in the mental well-being space, we identified four dominant approaches: content-first platforms, AI/chat-based companions, mood tracking & journaling tools, and gamified self-care systems.

Although these solutions support users through awareness, habits, and content, three major gaps became clear:

1

Engagement is high, but long-term meaning is weak.

Many apps succeed in bringing users back, but their suggestions often become repetitive over time and fail to connect meaningfully with users’ everyday lives.

2

Personalization often stays superficial.

These systems may collect data, but they often struggle to turn that data into support that feels relevant, contextual, and truly actionable for the individual user.

3

There is a gap between emotional support and real-life action.

Many solutions focus too much on content, tracking, or gamification.
What is missing is a clear bridge to translate a recognized feeling into a meaningful real-life action.

Literature Research

1What is Mental Well-being?

Mental well-being is more than happiness. It is the ability to feel balanced, cope with daily stress, and find meaning in life.
It includes hedonic well-being: pleasure and positive emotions, and eudaimonic well-being: meaning, autonomy, and personal growth.

2Social Media & Emotional Overload

A systematic review by Keles, McCrae, and Grealish (2020) found that adolescent social media use is associated with depression, anxiety, and psychological distress.

3Passive Scrolling & Social Comparison

Passive scrolling can increase social comparison and envy. A systematic review found that social comparison on social networking sites was consistently related to envy and depression.

4Emotional Awareness

Recognizing and naming emotions is a key step in self-regulation. Before people can cope with a feeling, they need to notice and define it.

5Small Actions & Behavioral Activation

Behavioral Activation is based on the idea that small, meaningful activities can improve mood by reducing avoidance and increasing engagement with daily life.

6Reflection & Journaling

Writing about experiences can help people organize thoughts, process emotions, and create meaning. A 2023 review of 24 randomized studies found positive writing had beneficial effects on mood.

Survey & Interviews

Mixed-method study combined a quantitative survey of 150+ users with deep-dive qualitative interviews to map the mental health.

Teddy's Data-Driven Response

  • Jar: Validates mental growth visually (addressing low self-esteem).
  • Tasks: Forces "Real-World" reconnection.
  • Journal: Space for self-expression

Friction & App Abandonment

Low vs. High Mental Health Profiles Coping Mechanisms Correlation

High cognitive load in self-care (e.g., long-form writing) correlates with a 3x higher dropout rate. Teddy’s low-friction journaling (voice/prompt) maximizes long-term user consistency.

When comparing groups with low (<=4) and high (>=7) mental health scores, sharp differences emerge in terms of lifestyle and stress factors:

User Group

Demographic

Age & Education: 18 – 25 years old | University students and young professionals.
Technology: Always-on smartphone users with extremely high daily screen time.

Behavioral Characteristics

Infinite Scrolling: Mindlessly scrolling through Reels/TikTok for hours, especially before bed.
FOMO & Comparison: Constantly benchmarking their lives against others' "perfect" feeds, leading to inadequacy.
Fake Breaks: Retreating into their phones when stressed rather than taking real-world breaks, resulting in short attention spans.

Motivations & Needs

Escaping Digital Pressure: Want to escape social media judgment; need a quiet, minimal space to reduce anxiety.
Returning to Real Life: Want to break screen addiction; need small, real-world prompts to step away from the phone.
Low-Effort Expression: Want to process emotions without pressure; need flexible options like text, voice, or guided prompts.
Encouraging Progress: Want to track well-being without clinical charts; need a warm, visual way to see personal growth.

App Design and Features

Home

The main control center where users can track their weekly emotion jar, complete daily wellbeing goals carried over from previous days, and revisit past similar moments through the "Felt Similar" section.

Feelings

Emotion entry screen that allows users to accurately identify and log their current mood using a wide pool of tags or an "emotion wheel" for guidance if they are unsure.

Journal

Space where users can view their weekly jar progress and log their inner reflections using their preferred method, such as free writing, guided prompts, voice notes, or photos.

teddy

An interactive chat interface where users can instantly message, vent, and receive support and guidance from an empathetic AI regarding their current feelings and thoughts.

Profile

Summary page where users can review their personal habits and statistics, including total entry count, active days, favorite journaling method, and the history of their emotion jars.
